Your Waste Carrier Registration Has an Expiry Date. Here’s What Replaces It

Anyone who has registered as a waste carrier knows how little it takes. Fill in a form, pay the fee, get a number. That ease is exactly what the government says criminals have been exploiting, and it is about to end.

The Environmental Permitting (Waste Controlling or Transporting) Regulations 2026 were made on 22 July this year. Most of the changes take effect twelve months later, on 22 July 2027. From then, the carrier, broker and dealer registration system in England is replaced by environmental permits, the same regime that already covers waste sites.

Out with carriers, brokers and dealers

The old labels are going. In their place are two roles:

  • Waste controllers – anyone who makes decisions about waste: arranging its movement, buying or selling it, or deciding where it goes. This includes brokers, dealers and consultants, even if they never touch the waste.
  • Waste transporters – anyone who physically moves it.

There are three permit types to match: controller only, transporter only, and a combined controller-transporter permit. A typical skip hire firm or man with a van who quotes the job, collects the waste and chooses where to tip it will most likely need the combined permit.

What you’ll have to prove

This is the big change. Registration simply recorded that you existed. A permit has to be earned.

Competence. You, or a nominated employee, will have to show you’re competent to transport waste or make decisions about where it goes. The CIWM is building the assessment with the Environment Agency, known as the Controllers and Transporters (CaT) Competence Scheme. It’s designed to be modular, so a driver won’t sit the same test as a broker.

Identity and criminal record checks. Applicants will be vetted. The Environment Agency can refuse a permit, and suspend or revoke one where conditions are broken.

Visible permit numbers. Your permit number will have to appear in your advertising. Drivers must carry the permit number and a unique driver number issued by their operator. That gives customers and enforcement officers a quick way to check whether whoever is collecting the waste is legitimate.

When it reaches you

Not everyone switches on day one.

  • 01Regulations made22 Jul 2026
  • 02New permitting regime starts22 Jul 2027
  • 03Upper-tier registrantsAt next renewal
  • 04Lower-tier registrantsA further 12 months

Upper-tier carriers, brokers and dealers will move across when their current three-year registration comes up for renewal. So depending on your renewal date, your switch could land any time in the three years after July 2027. Lower-tier registrants who need a permit or registered exemption generally get a further 12 months after the new system starts.

If you’re not sure which tier you’re on, now is the time to check your registration on the Environment Agency’s public register and note the expiry date.

The rules apply in England only. Wales is keeping its existing registration system for now.

What it’ll cost

Permit fees will cover processing and compliance monitoring. The detailed charges haven’t been published yet and are expected before the new system starts. The government’s own impact assessment puts the net cost to legitimate businesses at £28.6 million a year, against an estimated £68.6 million in benefits from cutting waste crime.

Why it matters even if you’re squeaky clean

It’s tempting to see this as a crackdown on rogue operators that doesn’t touch honest businesses. But a permit comes with conditions, and conditions can be checked. Combine that with digital waste tracking from October 2027 and the Environment Agency will be able to see who moved a load, who decided where it went, and whether they were allowed to.

The operators who’ll find the new regime easiest are the ones whose records are already complete and easy to produce. Clean, consistent waste transfer notes are the simplest place to start.
